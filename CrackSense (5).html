About CrackSense

Entropy

A measure of unpredictability in bits: characters × log₂(pool size). Every extra bit doubles the guesses. CrackSense counts predictable characters as almost zero, because attackers try them first.

Brute force vs dictionary attack

Brute force tries every combination, so length wins. A dictionary attack tries real words with common tweaks, so “P@ssw0rd2024” falls almost instantly even though it looks complex.

X-ray view

Highlights common words, sequences (abcd, 4321), keyboard walks (qwerty), repeats and years in red so you can see what to change.

Hashing and salting

History never stores your password. A random 16-byte salt is added, then PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 runs 100,000 times. Hashing is one-way, salt defeats rainbow tables, and the iterations make each guess slow.

Privacy

Everything runs locally in your browser. Nothing is sent to a server.
